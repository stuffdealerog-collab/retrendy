# TRIZ-40 deck

Altshuller's 40 inventive principles, translated from engineering into creative
moves. Original principle in brackets. TRIZ starts from a **contradiction**:
before using a card, state the brief's contradiction in one line ("it must be
intimate but reach millions", "it must be about loss but make people dance")
and use the card to resolve it rather than compromise.

## Cards

### T01 Split it (Segmentation)
Break the piece, the hero or the message into independent parts that work alone and add up differently (a story told in separate receipts; a hook made of three overheard fragments).

### T02 Take one thing out (Taking out)
Extract the single most charged element and drop everything around it. What is left when only the gesture, only the object, only the sound remains?

### T03 Make one part different (Local quality)
Give one part of a uniform structure a different rule: one verse in another tense, one frame in colour, one line spoken by someone else.

### T04 Break the symmetry (Asymmetry)
Where the form expects balance (call and response, before and after, two lovers), make it lopsided: one side talks, the other answers with an object.

### T05 Run things in parallel (Merging)
Make two simultaneous processes share one timeline: the breakup happens during a fire drill; the proposal happens during a software update.

### T06 One thing does many jobs (Universality)
One phrase, object or character carries several functions: the title is also an instruction, the prop is also the narrator.

### T07 Nest it (Nested doll)
Put a story inside a story inside an object: the song is quoted by a character in the song; the ad is an ad watched inside the ad.

### T08 Counterweight (Anti-weight)
Balance a heavy emotion with a light carrier, or a frivolous topic with a grave form: grief in a jingle, a shopping list read as an oath.

### T09 Pre-empt the objection (Preliminary anti-action)
Open with the audience's strongest objection or cynicism and build on top of it, instead of hoping they won't think it.

### T10 Do it in advance (Preliminary action)
Show the preparation instead of the event: rehearsing an apology, packing for a trip that never happens, the night before.

### T11 Cushion in advance (Beforehand cushioning)
Build in the failure from the start: the hero already knows it won't work; the product admits its limit upfront.

### T12 Keep it level (Equipotentiality)
Remove the climb: no build-up, no drop, the same intensity throughout, and find meaning in the flatness.

### T13 The other way round (Inversion)
Invert the action, the roles or the value: the object leaves the person, the city misses the tourist, the reward punishes.

### T14 Curve it (Spheroidality)
Replace linear progression with a loop or spiral: the ending returns to the first line changed; the story repeats with one variable shifted.

### T15 Make it adjustable (Dynamics)
Let something change its rules mid-piece: the narrator's reliability, the meaning of the refrain, the genre.

### T16 Do slightly less or much more (Partial or excessive action)
If the full thing is impossible, do a deliberately partial version (half a confession) or an absurd surplus (a thousand thank-yous).

### T17 Change dimension (Another dimension)
Move from a line to a plane, from story to map, from 2D to 3D: tell it as a floor plan, a timetable, a family tree, a cross-section.

### T18 Vibrate (Mechanical vibration)
Use rhythmic oscillation as content: alternate two states line by line (yes/no, here/there), so the alternation itself is the emotion.

### T19 Pulse it (Periodic action)
Replace continuous action with pulses: the same moment shown every year, every Tuesday, every time the elevator stops.

### T20 Never stop (Continuity of useful action)
Make something that never pauses carry the piece: a conveyor, a queue, a heartbeat monitor, a loading bar.

### T21 Rush through (Skipping)
Pass through the dangerous or boring part so fast it becomes a device: a whole relationship in one breath, a life in a list.

### T22 Turn harm into benefit (Blessing in disguise)
Take the brief's biggest negative (the product's flaw, the audience's bad habit, the topic's taboo) and make it the hook.

### T23 Close the loop (Feedback)
Let the audience's reaction change the piece, or show the piece reacting to itself: the song hears that it is being skipped.

### T24 Add a go-between (Intermediary)
Route the message through an intermediary: a translator, a courier, a parrot, a voice assistant, a lawyer.

### T25 Let it serve itself (Self-service)
The thing explains, sells, repairs or mourns itself: an object writes its own obituary, the jingle advertises the jingle.

### T26 Use a copy (Copying)
Work with a replica, imitation or reflection instead of the original: the cover band, the knock-off, the stand-in, the screenshot.

### T27 Cheap and short-lived (Cheap short-living objects)
Build the piece on disposable things: receipts, tickets, sticky notes, autocorrect suggestions, tea-bag tags.

### T28 Swap the sense (Mechanics substitution)
Replace the expected sense or channel with another one: describe a sight through sound, a feeling through temperature, a person through their smell.

### T29 Make it fluid (Pneumatics and hydraulics)
Use pressure, flow and leaks as the image system: what fills, what drains, what bursts.

### T30 Thin skin (Flexible shells and thin films)
Focus on surfaces and membranes: packaging, screens, wallpaper, skin, the thin wall between neighbours.

### T31 Add holes (Porous materials)
Leave deliberate gaps the audience fills: missing words, redacted lines, unanswered calls, empty frames.

### T32 Change the colour (Colour changes)
Mark change with a single visible property switching: the light changes, the font changes, one word is replaced each time.

### T33 Same material (Homogeneity)
Build everything from the same material as the subject: a song about a city made only of its street sounds, an ad made only of customer complaints.

### T34 Use it up and bring it back (Discarding and recovering)
Something is thrown away mid-piece and returns transformed at the end.

### T35 Change a parameter (Parameter changes)
Change one physical or social parameter radically: temperature, age, speed, volume, distance, status.

### T36 Phase change (Phase transitions)
Find the moment when one state becomes another (ice to water, crush to love, guest to family) and make the piece only that moment.

### T37 Expansion (Thermal expansion)
Show how something grows or shrinks under pressure: a lie expanding, a room shrinking, a word swelling with each repetition.

### T38 Accelerate (Strong oxidants)
Put the story under a catalyst that speeds everything up: a countdown, a deadline, a battery at 2 %.

### T39 Neutral environment (Inert atmosphere)
Place a charged event in the most neutral setting possible (a waiting room, a spreadsheet, a corporate email) and let the contrast work.

### T40 Composite (Composite materials)
Assemble the piece from mismatched materials with distinct properties: a collage of formats, voices or eras that holds together by one rule.
