# SCAMPER deck

Seven transformations (Osborn's checklist, as structured by Eberle). Apply a
card to one **specific element** of the brief: the speaker, the addressee, the
object, the setting, the form, the moment in time, the audience's ritual. Each
card lists moves; pick one and push it until it stops being polite.

## Cards

### SC1 Substitute
- Replace the expected speaker with someone or something that should not be speaking.
- Replace the expected setting with one that has opposite rules (silence where there is noise, bureaucracy where there is romance).
- Replace the central emotion with a neighbouring one that is rarely expressed (relief instead of grief, envy instead of love).
- Replace the medium the story usually travels through (letter → delivery tracking, phone call → elevator intercom).

### SC2 Combine
- Fuse the brief's subject with an unrelated routine procedure so that the procedure carries the emotion.
- Merge two audiences who never meet into one scene.
- Make one object do two jobs in the story (the gift is also the evidence).
- Combine a high register with a low one in the same line (liturgy + grocery list).

### SC3 Adapt
- Borrow the structure of a genre from another field (sports commentary, safety briefing, auction, weather forecast) and fill it with the brief's content.
- Adapt a solution nature uses for the same problem (camouflage, migration, molting).
- Adapt how a child, a machine or a foreigner would misunderstand the situation, and make the misunderstanding the point.

### SC4 Modify (magnify / minify)
- Shrink the scale to one tiny object or one second and treat it as epic.
- Blow a trivial detail up to absurd importance, seriously.
- Change the duration: compress a decade into one take, stretch one minute into a whole piece.
- Exaggerate one property until it becomes a rule of the world.

### SC5 Put to another use
- Use the brief's product, feeling or ritual for a purpose it was never meant for.
- Make the audience's waste (leftovers, abandoned things, deleted messages) the material.
- Let the piece itself have a function in the world (a song that is also a password, an ad that is also a form to fill).

### SC6 Eliminate
- Remove the element everyone considers essential (the hero, the product shot, the chorus, the word "love") and build around the hole.
- Remove all but one sense (only sounds, only smells).
- Cut the explanation: start after the event, end before the resolution.

### SC7 Reverse / rearrange
- Tell it backwards: the ending first, the cause last.
- Swap roles: the object watches the person, the audience performs for the brand, the listener sings to the singer.
- Invert the value: celebrate what is usually mourned, regret what is usually celebrated.
- Put the climax at the beginning and make the rest an aftermath.
