# Oblique prompts deck

Lateral prompts in the spirit of Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt's *Oblique
Strategies* (1975). These are **original prompts written for this plugin**,
not the published card texts (those are copyrighted). The method is the same:
a prompt that is deliberately not about the problem forces you out of the
frame you are stuck in. Do not interpret a card literally if a looser reading
gives a stranger and more specific idea.

## Cards

### O01 The mistake is the brief
Take the first error, typo or misunderstanding you notice in the brief or your draft and treat it as intentional.

### O02 Ask the furniture
Which object in the scene has been present the whole time and never spoken? Let it decide.

### O03 Lower the volume
What would the idea be if it had to work whispered, at 20 % intensity?

### O04 The boring cousin
Find the most boring possible version of the idea, then find the one thing in it that is secretly interesting.

### O05 Who leaves the room?
Remove the person the idea is built around. What do the others do now?

### O06 Wrong tool
Make it with the wrong tool: a love song with a spreadsheet, an ad with a legal disclaimer, a name with a barcode.

### O07 Last line first
Write the final moment first and refuse to explain how we got there.

### O08 Repeat until it changes
Repeat one element until repetition alters its meaning.

### O09 The neighbour's view
Describe the event from the flat next door, through the wall.

### O10 Make it smaller than possible
Find the smallest unit that still carries the whole feeling.

### O11 Unfinished on purpose
Stop the idea one step before it resolves and leave the gap visible.

### O12 Translate it badly
Imagine the idea translated into another language and back by a cheap machine. Keep the strange part.

### O13 What is it ashamed of?
Every subject has an embarrassing side it hides. Lead with that side.

### O14 Remove the obvious word
Ban the one word everybody would use for this topic. Build the idea without it.

### O15 Hire an amateur
How would someone with no skill in this field, but complete sincerity, do it?

### O16 Change the clock
Move the event to the least likely hour of the day or season of the year.

### O17 A detail from your pocket
Take a concrete, unglamorous object (a receipt, a key, a charging cable) and make it carry the meaning.

### O18 Disagree with the brief
State the strongest disagreement with the brief's assumption. Is there an idea in being right about it?

### O19 What does the audience do at 7 a.m.?
Ignore the topic; observe the audience's most routine moment, then connect it back.

### O20 Two speeds
Run one part extremely slow and another extremely fast at the same time.

### O21 Keep the scaffolding
Leave the construction visible: the draft notes, the stage directions, the counting-in.

### O22 Instead of a hero, a procedure
Replace the protagonist with a process (a queue, a form, a recipe, a firmware update).

### O23 The sound of the room
What does the place sound like when nothing happens? Start there.

### O24 Take the side of the loser
Tell it from the side that loses, without pity.

### O25 Make one thing impossible
Allow exactly one impossible element and keep everything else painfully realistic.

### O26 What would be cheaper?
The budget just went to zero. What is the version that costs nothing and loses nothing important?

### O27 Withhold the payoff
Promise something and deliberately never deliver it; make the absence the payoff.

### O28 Write it as a rule
Turn the idea into a rule, law or instruction someone has to follow.

### O29 Use the margin
Move the important content to the margin: the footnote, the fine print, the credits, the background.

### O30 Old person, new thing
Put a very old perspective on a very new phenomenon, or the reverse.

### O31 Too honest
Have someone say the truest, least strategic thing possible in that situation.

### O32 Swap the stakes
Make the big thing trivial and the trivial thing life-or-death.

### O33 Count something
Find something countable in the situation and let the numbers tell the story.

### O34 The second time
Show not the first time something happens but the second, when everyone knows how it goes.

### O35 Give it a job
The idea has to clock in and do a job. What job is it?

### O36 Leave the edges rough
Pick the most polished part and make it rough; pick the roughest and polish it.
