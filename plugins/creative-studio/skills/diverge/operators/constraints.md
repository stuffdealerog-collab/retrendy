# Constraints deck

Formal constraints in the Oulipo tradition plus production constraints. A
constraint removes the default solution and makes you find a second one,
which is where originality lives. Apply the drawn constraint **seriously** to
the concept (and to the sample), then state in `escalation` what it forced you
to discover. You may relax it in the final versions later; the discovery stays.

## Cards

### K01 One room
Everything happens in one small, specific room. No flashbacks outside it.

### K02 One take, one minute
The whole piece must fit one continuous minute of real time.

### K03 No people visible
No human appears or speaks directly; only traces of people.

### K04 Lipogram of the key word
The topic's central word (and its synonyms) never appears.

### K05 Only questions
Every line or beat is a question.

### K06 Only nouns carry it
Build the core from a list of concrete nouns; verbs and adjectives only where unavoidable.

### K07 Second person singular
The whole piece addresses one specific "you" who is not who we first assume.

### K08 Found text
Assemble the key material from existing everyday text forms: signs, labels, notifications, forms (invented, not real brands).

### K09 Numbers only
The hook or central device must be built on a number.

### K10 Reverse chronology
Events appear from last to first.

### K11 Three objects
The idea may use only three props. Each must appear at least twice.

### K12 Snowball
Each beat or line is longer than the previous one by one word or one element.

### K13 Same opening every time
Every section starts with the same phrase, and the phrase means something different each time.

### K14 Forbidden channel
Ban the main channel the topic usually lives in (no phone in a breakup, no beach in a summer piece, no screen in a tech ad).

### K15 Mundane register
Write the most emotional moment in the most bureaucratic language possible.

### K16 Single sense
Only one sense is allowed (sound, smell, touch, taste or sight).

### K17 Unreliable narrator
The speaker gets one fact wrong, and the audience notices before the speaker does.

### K18 Zero budget
The production costs nothing: no locations, no actors, no effects. What remains?

### K19 Child's vocabulary
Only words a nine-year-old uses, for an adult subject.

### K20 One-word hook
The memorable centre is a single word.

### K21 Opposite genre
Deliver the content in the genre that is most wrong for it (lullaby for anger, march for doubt, cooking show for heartbreak).

### K22 Real time of an object
Tell the story at the speed of an object's life: a candle burning, milk spoiling, a phone battery draining.

### K23 Two voices, no dialogue
Two speakers alternate but never respond to each other directly.

### K24 Exactly 50 words
The core of the concept (logline + concept) must be expressible in exactly 50 words.

### K25 Index card
The idea must be explained to a stranger with a single drawing and five words.

### K26 Ending known upfront
The title or the first beat gives away the ending; the interest is in how.

### K27 No metaphors
Every line is literal. Find the emotion in plain fact.

### K28 Mirror form
The second half repeats the first half in reverse order, with changed meaning.

### K29 Ban the climax
Nothing dramatic is allowed to happen; the drama is entirely in what we notice.

### K30 Borrowed rhythm
The rhythm or structure is copied from a non-artistic sequence (a phone number, a countdown, a bus route, a recipe's steps).
