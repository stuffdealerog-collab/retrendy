# Domain pack: song lyrics

Read by the orchestrator, generators, critic and editor. Craft rules live in
`craft.md`, clichés in `cliche-bank.md`.

## What a "concept" is here

A song concept is **the decision that makes this song different from the other
songs on the same theme**. It is not a theme ("a song about loneliness") and not
a mood. It has five parts:

| Field | What it holds | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| `title` | Working title, ideally the hook phrase | "Одиночество" | "Автоответчик Минздрава" |
| `logline` | One sentence: who is singing, to whom, in what situation | "Песня о расставании" | "Диспетчер такси диктует адреса бывшей, не называя её" |
| `concept` | 2-4 sentences: the central device (POV, frame, image system, structural trick) and why it hits the emotion sideways | "Герой грустит и вспоминает" | "Каждый куплет — новый адрес; припев — одна и та же фраза „машина подана“, которая к третьему разу означает „я сдаюсь“" |
| `sample` | 4-8 lines: the hook + a verse fragment that proves the device works in actual language | abstract emotion words | concrete nouns, singable rhythm |
| `operator` | Which card or move produced it | | "TRIZ-13 Inversion" |

Optional fields: `genre_hint` (genre or sonic reference described in words,
never "in the style of <artist>"), `language`.

## Where originality usually lives in lyrics

Use these as directions to push toward, not as templates:

1. **Point of view**: an unexpected singer (an object, an institution, a
   bystander, the "you" of a classic love song answering back, a future self,
   the song itself).
2. **Frame**: a non-song text form carrying the song (instruction manual,
   court transcript, weather report, recipe, terms of service, a shopping list,
   a delivery-tracking notification).
3. **Image system**: one concrete, unusual semantic field carried through the
   whole song instead of mixed generic metaphors.
4. **Structural device**: the chorus changes meaning each time it returns; the
   verses are in reverse chronological order; the hook is a mis-heard phrase;
   the bridge is a different speaker.
5. **Specificity**: a real-feeling detail (brand-free) that no other song would
   have: a crossword clue, a broken intercom code, the name on a lost-and-found
   tag.
6. **Emotional angle**: the "wrong" emotion for the situation (relief at a
   breakup, boredom at a party, jealousy toward a houseplant) played sincerely.

## Trend angles (for trend-radar)

Lyrics react to culture slower than short video but faster than novels. Useful
signals:

- vocabulary: new slang, memes that became phrases, institutional or tech
  language entering everyday speech;
- situations: new everyday rituals (new apps, new kinds of work, new
  loneliness);
- sonic and format trends: song length, sped-up/slowed edits, hooks built for
  15-second clips;
- counter-signals: what audiences say they are tired of hearing about.

Seed queries for web search, adapt them to the brief's language and market:
`"new slang <year>"`, `"songs about <topic> trend"`, `"<platform> sound trend
<month year>"`, `"why are all songs about"`, `"lyrics people are tired of"`.

## Output of the editor (versions)

For each finalist the editor returns three versions (see
`skills/converge/SKILL.md`):

- `title`: title of this version;
- `hook`: 2-4 lines, singable, the phrase people would quote;
- `verse`: 4-8 lines that set up the device;
- `note`: one or two sentences on delivery, structure or production, in words
  (tempo, arrangement idea), no artist names.

## Taboo defaults

Unless the brief says otherwise, avoid:

- imitating a named living artist's style or quoting existing lyrics;
- real people as targets;
- slurs, including "reclaimed" ones.
