# Craft rules: song lyrics

Used by generators (to write the `sample`), the critic (the `craft` criterion)
and the editor (versions). Each rule has a check you can apply to a line.

## Language

1. **Concrete over abstract.** Every line needs at least one noun you can see,
   touch or hear. Check: underline the nouns. If they are "love", "pain",
   "soul", "heart", "dream" or "eternity", rewrite.
2. **Show the emotion through behaviour.** Instead of "I'm lonely": what the
   person does at 7 p.m. on a Tuesday. Check: does the line name the feeling?
   Then it is probably telling, not showing.
3. **One image system per song.** Do not mix ocean, fire and flight in one
   chorus. Check: list the metaphors; they should come from one field.
4. **Specific beats general.** "The intercom code 47K" beats "your door". The
   detail should feel real without being a brand name.
5. **Speech you can actually say.** Read the line aloud: no inverted word order
   just to rhyme. In Russian especially, watch for unnatural constructions
   built only to end on a rhyme.

## Sound

6. **Prosody.** Stressed syllables fall on strong beats; the hook's key word
   lands on the longest note. Check: clap the rhythm; the clap should land on
   a stressed syllable.
7. **Fresh rhyme over perfect rhyme.** Slant, assonant and internal rhymes are
   better than a "perfect" rhyme from the cliché bank (`{rhyme}` entries).
   Check: if you can predict the rhyme word before reading it, change it.
8. **Vowels for long notes.** Open vowels (а, о / ah, oh) on sustained notes;
   avoid clusters of consonants on high notes.

## Structure

9. **The hook earns its repetition.** Each return of the chorus means a bit
   more because the verse before it changed the context. Check: does verse 2
   change how the hook reads?
10. **Verse = information, chorus = feeling, bridge = turn.** The bridge must
    reveal or reverse something, not just repeat the chorus quieter (see
    LY-082).
11. **The first line is a camera position.** It tells us where we are and who
    is speaking within one line.
12. **Title test.** The title should be a phrase someone would type into
    search after hearing the song once.

## Scoring the `craft` criterion (1-5)

- **1**: abstract emotion words, mixed metaphors, rhymes from the cliché bank,
  unsingable.
- **2**: some concrete detail, but generic rhyme and imagery dominate.
- **3**: a consistent image system and singable lines; no surprises in the
  language.
- **4**: specific details, fresh rhymes, a hook that works out of context.
- **5**: every line carries the device; the hook changes meaning on return;
  you would quote it.

Evidence for craft is always a quoted line from `sample`.
